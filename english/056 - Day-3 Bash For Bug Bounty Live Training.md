- **QUOTING — definition:** "just a set of characters" — a mechanism that **affects practically every part of how bash processes the command line**. Rationale: special characters (`$`, `&`, `*`, spaces…) were made *for special purposes*; quoting = **removing a character's special meaning** when you want it literal. Missing this concept = mysterious errors in daily commands and, worse, in your own scripts.
- **The THREE quoting types (screenshot-worthy, his words):**
  1. **Backslash `\`** — removes the special meaning of the character **immediately following** it.
  2. **Single quotes `'…'`** — removes the special meaning of **every** enclosed character (stated by exclusion across his loops; the double-quote's exceptions are what make singe-quotes total).
  3. **Double quotes `"…"`** — removes special meaning of every enclosed character **EXCEPT the `$` and backtick** (variable expansion & command substitution still fire inside). **Built-in flexibility:** `\` still works inside double quotes — so `\$` prints a literal dollar even in double quotes, letting expansion and literalness mix in one string.
  - Terminology: once a character's special meaning is removed, it's **escaped** ("escape").
- **Demo 1 — the ampersand accident:** `echo Sachin & Nitesh` — `&` = "run in background," so bash backgrounds `echo Sachin` and parses `Nitesh` as a **second command** (which fails/executes oddly). Expected text never prints. **Fix:** `echo Sachin \& Nitesh` → prints `Sachin & Nitesh` verbatim. Lesson stated: **one special character cost me my intended output.**
- **Demo 2 — same healing inside a variable assignment:** `user=Sachin\ \&\ Nitesh` — backslashes on the ampersand *and* the intervening spaces (both special to the parser) → variable stores exactly `Sachin & Nitesh`; echoing it reproduces the text. (= "quoted/escaped" storage.)
